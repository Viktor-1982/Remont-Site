# Types of Modular Homes: Complete Guide to Prefab Living & Costs (2026)

Modular housing has transformed from temporary budget shelters into cutting-edge architectural marvels. Engineered in climate-controlled factory settings and transported to the job site for precision craning, the **different types of modular homes** offer unprecedented build speed, high thermal performance, and strict quality control.

Whether you are evaluating a compact guest dwelling or an expansive multi-story family residence, understanding the distinct types of modular homes—their structural frameworks, building materials, and realistic cost benchmarks—is essential before choosing a manufacturer.

![Modern modular home exterior](/images/vidy-modulnykh-domov/modern-modular-home.webp)

## What Is a Modular Home

A **modular home** is a prefabricated building assembled from factory-made blocks (modules).  
Each module is produced with interior finishing, utilities, and layout in place —  
reducing construction time and ensuring consistent quality in any season.

![Assembly of modular home sections](/images/vidy-modulnykh-domov/assembly.webp)

---

## Main Construction Types

### Frame Modular Homes
Built on a wooden or metal frame, insulated and clad with modern materials.  
Lightweight, warm, and durable with proper care.

![Wood frame modular home structure](/images/vidy-modulnykh-domov/frame-house.webp)

**Advantages:**
- fast assembly;
- energy efficiency;
- easy maintenance;
- reliable structure.

---

### Block-Modular Homes
Each block is fully manufactured at the factory and delivered ready for installation —  
complete with windows, doors, and interior finishing.

![Factory-assembled block modular house](/images/vidy-modulnykh-domov/block-house.webp)

**Benefits:**
- quick assembly;
- high precision;
- minimal on-site work;
- easy expansion.

---

### Container Homes
Made from repurposed shipping containers.  
Strong, mobile, and industrial in style — often used for offices, cafés, or seasonal housing.

![Container modular home](/images/vidy-modulnykh-domov/container-home.webp)

## Materials Used

### Wood
Eco-friendly, warm, and aesthetically natural. Needs protective treatment against moisture and fire.  
![Wood modular home exterior](/images/vidy-modulnykh-domov/wood-house.webp)

### Metal
Durable and fire-resistant — suitable for commercial and northern climates.  
Requires anti-corrosion coating.  
![Metal modular home](/images/vidy-modulnykh-domov/metal-house.webp)

### Composites (Sandwich Panels)
Lightweight, sealed, and energy-efficient.  
Easy to transport, assemble, and maintain.  
![Composite sandwich panels](/images/vidy-modulnykh-domov/composite-panels.webp)

## Sizes and Purpose

- **Compact (under 270 sq ft / 25 sq m)** — Ideal for accessory dwelling units (ADUs), guest houses, studio backyard offices, or glamping retreats.

- **Medium (320–750 sq ft / 30–70 sq m)** — Two-to-three module configurations perfect for couples, small families, or secondary vacation residences.

- **Large (860+ sq ft / 80+ sq m)** — Multi-story, multi-module residential layouts designed for full-time family living with complete master suites and open-plan living.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### What are the main types of modular homes?
The primary types of modular homes include wood-frame modulars (built like traditional homes in factory sections), volumetric block homes (fully finished room modules placed by crane), steel shipping container homes, and panelized SIP (Structural Insulated Panel) systems.

### Are modular homes cheaper than traditional stick-built homes?
Yes. On average, modular homes are **15% to 25% cheaper** than conventional on-site construction. Factory mass purchasing, zero weather-induced construction delays, and minimized labor waste reduce total project overhead while delivering tighter building tolerances.

### How long do modern modular homes last?
When placed on a permanent foundation (such as concrete stem walls or a crawl space), modular homes last **50 to 100+ years**—identical to site-built houses. They must meet the same local International Residential Code (IRC) or national building standards as traditional stick-built construction.
